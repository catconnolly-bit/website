In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth. The stars and planets, the clouds and mountains, the oceans with their waves and currents. All things lived and moved and had their being through the word of our Creator, who formed everything out of nothing and called it good.

As history unfolded, God called a people. God promised Abraham descendants as numerous as the stars, wrestled Jacob through a long night, and led Israel through the waters of the Red Sea into freedom. Over the centuries, faith came and went. People remembered and forgot, worshiped and turned away. Yet time and again, God showed Godself to be present.

Then one day Moses stood before a burning bush, looking for answers. ‘Who am I to speak to Pharaoh? What shall I say when I am asked who sent me?’ And from the fire came a voice, giving words to unmeasurable divinity.

I AM.

I AM WHO I AM. Ever present. Unchanging. Awe and power and faithfulness beyond measure. It was not an explanation Moses could offer to Pharoah. Instead it was a promise: I will be with you. I was with you, am with you, will be with you. Before Pharaoh, at the sea, through the wilderness, into the unknown that lies ahead: I AM. I AM who was and is and is to come.

Generations later, Elijah came to Horeb, the mountain of God, and hid in a cave. He is worn out, frightened, defensive, but God’s faithful presence speaks to him too. No matter that people have done everything wrong. No matter that God’s prophet himself is at a point of despair. I AM is still I AM. There through all things, even whispering beneath sheer silence.

The earth spins on its axis, centuries go by, and we arrive at another night.

The wind is wild, whipping wet hair into eyes. The boat has been flung violently on the waves for hours. Water spills over the edge and swirls around exhausted feet. The shore has long since disappeared into darkness. Even for those who have spent their lives in boats, the limit of skill is being tested.The struggle has been going on all night, and the wind is still continuing unabated.

Then someone sees movement across the water. A figure comes toward them through the darkness, walking where no one can walk. Of course in their terror there is only one explanation: a ghost. The poor disciples cry out. Perhaps for a moment they wonder how to steer the boat in the opposite direction. But above the wind and the water and fear, a voice speaks. One they know. One they trust. Impossibly near when they are impossibly far from land.

"Take heart. I AM. Do not be afraid."

Our English translation says, "It is I." But the Greek words are: I AM.

There is no comparison this time. Not bread or vine or door. Simply presence: ‘I am here. The sea cannot keep me from you. Darkness cannot hide you from me. Wind cannot carry my voice away.’ What Moses heard beside the burning bush, the disciples hear above the water.

I AM.

Behind those words are all the waters of Scripture. The Spirit moving over the deep. The Red Sea opening into freedom. The psalmists singing of the One whose path runs through the oceans and whose voice stills the storm. Now those memories gather around Jesus as the water holds his footsteps.

Throughout all of history, we have told ourselves stories. Stories of our own lives, of where we succeed or fail. Stories of what makes us who we are, how we ended up here, and why we are experiencing our current reality. We also tell stories of God - where God is or is not to be found; what faith looks like, what the requirements or correct beliefs might be. But if the Bible tells us anything, it is that God is unshakeable. No matter if it is through a storm, in a fire, at the depths of despair or the heights of joy, close at hand or seemingly ungraspable, or a quiet voice in the midst of utter silence, what holds true is that God IS.

I wonder how often you might feel that God is distant or absent. I wonder if you’ve ever felt overwhelmed by a situation or circumstances. I wonder if you have ever felt alone.

But our readings today highlight the overarching theme of our faith, that God never abandons us. And our saving might look very different to what we expect, and different for us than for our neighbour, but Paul assures us in the letter to the Romans that everyone who calls on the name of the Lord shall be saved. Peter gives us a very concrete example of what that looks like, in a moment of panic and fear, crying out, ‘Lord, save me!’ And Jesus does. The I AM catches him, steps back into the boat with him, and in that moment as the storm dies away the disciples finally know and acknowledge who Jesus really is: "Truly you are the Son of God."

In the days that follow, another darkness will fall in Jerusalem, and at the foot of the cross another voice will confess, ‘Truly this was the Son of God’. But even when faced with death itself, I AM is still I AM, and nothing can remove God’s presence. Perhaps that is our greatest comfort. Death still frightens us, we hide from it and do our best to avoid thinking about it, but still in the face of its inevitability we know that it is no match for our Creator who is always, always there.

So whatever now holds for you, whatever the future holds for you, for each of us, for the Episcopal Church, for this country, there are some things we know with certainty: that the Gospel is the good news of God among all people. That places, languages and people change, but God’s faithful presence remains. That the story is always being told, and that at its heart remains the I AM who was and is and is to come.

So when the wind is against us and our strength is spent, when the story we are telling ourselves has narrowed to fear or failure or loneliness, faith may be no more complicated than Peter’s prayer:

“Lord, save me.” And the answer of God is the answer that has echoed through the whole long story of Scripture.

I AM. I am here. I was here before you knew to look for me. I am here in the storm you cannot control. I will be here in whatever comes next.

The story we tell ourselves is never the whole story, because God is always larger than the limits of what we can see. So take heart. The God who spoke from the burning bush, whispered to Elijah, walked upon the sea, endured the cross, and broke open the grave is with us still.

And because God is, I AM WHO I AM, we need not be afraid.

Amen.

1 Kings 19:9-18;  Romans 10:5-15;  Matthew 14:22-33
